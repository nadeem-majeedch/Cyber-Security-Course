# Case-Study Assessment Rubrics

Cases are primarily formative (classroom method, ungraded reasoning practice). Where graded (checkpoints, participation, capstone integration), use these rubrics. All rubrics reward *argued reasoning and named tradeoffs*, never the "right answer."

## Rubric A — In-class participation (per case, 4 pts)

| Dimension | 1 pt | 2 pts |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence use | Restates scenario | Reasons *from* the available evidence; flags what's missing |
| Reasoning | Assertion without mechanism | Explains *why* (mechanism, attack path, control logic) |
| Tradeoffs | Single solution, no costs | Names at least one cost/limitation of own proposal |
| Engagement | Passive | Builds on peer answers; asks a discriminating question |

## Rubric B — Written case response (Level 1–2, 10 pts)

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| Correct identification of the core risk/flaw (with CIA/property or CWE-class reasoning) | 3 |
| Solution appropriateness (matches constraints, follows defense hierarchy) | 3 |
| Alternative considered + tradeoff named | 2 |
| Assumptions stated | 1 |
| Clarity/structure (decision first, then reasoning) | 1 |

## Rubric C — Written case response (Level 3–4, 20 pts)

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| Framing: stakeholders, constraints, and success criteria correctly scoped | 4 |
| Reasoning quality: mechanisms, evidence use, valid inference (no invented facts) | 5 |
| Solution quality: actionable, prioritized, feasible under stated constraints | 4 |
| Tradeoffs & alternatives: ≥ 2 alternatives with honest costs; residual risk named | 4 |
| Assumptions & limitations: explicit, material, challenged where warranted | 2 |
| Communication: decision-first structure; concise; executable by the stated audience | 1 |

**Open-ended cases (most of Level 3–4):** any defensible position earns full marks when its tradeoffs are honest and its assumptions stated; the instructor key's "reference position" is one strong answer, not the answer.

## Rubric D — Group presentation of reasoning (step 3 of the method, 5 pts)

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| Clear decision/position stated up front | 1 |
| Reasoning chain traceable (evidence → inference → conclusion) | 2 |
| Handles one instructor challenge without collapsing (revises honestly if wrong) | 1 |
| Division of reasoning among group members (not one voice) | 1 |

## Grading-integrity notes

- Reward *changed minds with reasons*: students who revise after peer discussion demonstrate the method working.
- Penalize invented facts (evidence not in the scenario) gently in-class, but flag the pattern — it is Rubric C's reasoning-quality axis.
- The instructor-only keys list "common mistakes" per case — these map directly to rubric deductions.
