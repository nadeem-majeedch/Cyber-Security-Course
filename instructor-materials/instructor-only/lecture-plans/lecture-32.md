# Lecture 32 — Capstone Showcase and Final Examination
**Module M8 · Week 16, Session 2 · 120 min · CLO-8 (primary) · CLO-1 (supporting)**

## Learning Objectives
1. Present and defend the capstone: threat model, controls, evidence, tabletop findings — under examiner questioning.
2. Critique peer defenses constructively (rubric-anchored peer review).
3. Complete the final examination covering CLO-1..8 (integration items, not recall).
4. Close the course: reflection, next-step pathways, and the ethics recommitment.

## Key Concepts
- Defense format: 6-minute presentation + 4-minute questions per team; rubric-anchored scoring
- Evidence hierarchy: live demo > artifact citation > narrative claim; examiners probe the weakest link
- Integration questions: cross-module scenarios (e.g., "your L06 fix meets an L23-style attacker — now what?")
- Course synthesis: security as layered engineering + measurement + ethics

## Examples
- **CS track:** a team demonstrates the parameterized search + the narrowed IAM role + the fired beacon alert in one 6-minute arc — the integration the rubric rewards at the top band.
- **DS track:** a team shows their phishing classifier's PR curve, the adversarial test (L23) they ran against themselves, and the mitigation they shipped — metanoia as evidence.

## Discussion Questions (during Q&A of defenses)
1. Which control would you remove if the budget halved — and what compensates?
2. Where does your system still fail? (every strong answer names a real one)
3. What would you instrument next and why?

## Student Activity
Presentations + defenses; structured peer review (rubric-anchored); final exam; retrospective.

## Problem-Solving Scenario (exam's centerpiece, summary form)
> A cross-module scenario: phish → credential → cloud role → exfil, with the team asked to (a) map ATT&CK techniques, (b) name two controls per stage with evidence they'd collect, (c) draft the first three IR decisions, (d) note the privacy duty triggered. This single item samples CLO-1..8.

## Summary
The showcase is the course's proof: students who can model, build, detect, respond, and account for their choices. The exam checks integration; the reflection checks humility. Graduates leave authorized-minded, defense-focused, and evidence-driven.

## Formative Assessment
Final examination; peer-review forms; one-minute retrospective.
