# Teaching Guide — Lecture 32 (Capstone Showcase + Final Exam)
**Instructor-only.**

## Timing Plan (120 min)

| Time | Segment | Delivery notes |
|---|---|---|
| 00–08 | Logistics | Rubric reminder; peer forms distributed; order of teams. |
| 08–68 | Showcase round | ~7 teams × 10 min (6+4). Strict timing. |
| 68–75 | Break + exam setup | A/B seating; papers staged. |
| 75–110 | **Final examination** | 35 min, integrated scenario. |
| 110–115 | Retrospective | One-minute papers; CLO self-assessment. |
| 115–120 | Close | Pathways; ethics recommitment; thanks. |

## Board/Projector Activities

- **Projector:** the timing clock (6:00/4:00 visible per team); team presentations from their own machines.
- **Peer forms:** rubric-anchored — one strength, one gap, one question the panel should ask.

## Speaker Notes (key beats)

1. The evidence hierarchy governs Q&A: probe the weakest evidence link; the top band *names residual risks unprompted*.
2. Strict timing is fairness: a visible clock, a timekeeper, and the rule that Q&A time is not presentation time.
3. Scoring during the round, not after — memory of ten defenses in one sitting is a grading hazard.
4. The exam is integration, not recall: one scenario crossing M1–M7; the coverage map was published in advance (`assessments/student/`).
5. Close with the ethics recommitment — the L01 contract, signed in spirit at the end as well as the start.

## Expected Student Difficulties

- Presentations over-run. Fix: the clock + a 30-second warning card; the discipline is part of the professional skill.
- Teams deflect residual-risk questions. Fix: the rubric rewards naming them; the panel asks "where does your system still fail?" explicitly.
- Exam anxiety after performing. Fix: the break is real; the coverage map was published; scenario format was rehearsed by every checkpoint and the midterm.

## Teaching Tips

- Score sheets per team *before* the session; write during Q&A, finalize during the next team's setup.
- Keep the exam's A/B variants in the instructor-only tier until the moment of distribution; collected papers return to that tier immediately.
- The retrospective one-minute papers are next term's first editing task — collect them; read them.

## Answer Keys **[KEY]**

- Showcase rubric (summary): integration arc /4; evidence quality /4; detection demo /2; tabletop findings honesty /2; defense under questioning /4; peer-form average /4. Bands: 16–20 top, 12–15 middle, <12 bottom with specific comments required.
- Final exam: papers + marking scheme in `assessments/instructor-only/` (A/B variants; the cross-module scenario samples CLO-1..8; marking guide includes partial-credit tables).
- Retrospective: no key — read for course-improvement themes; tag recurring items as `content-bug`/`content-improve` issues per the manual §10.

## Lab Delivery

No lab; the showcase *is* the practical assessment. Environment should stay live until after the final showcase Q&A (demos may need it).

## Discussion Facilitation

Panel Q&A: three prepared questions per team, chosen from their artifacts (the weakest evidence link, the biggest residual risk, the detection they trust least). Uniform depth across teams keeps grading fair.

## Accessibility

- Presentations: seated presenting allowed; captioning for any embedded video; the timing clock is visual *and* verbal.
- Exam: extended-time room and large-print/screen-reader formats prepared per manual §6; A/B variants support separate-room invigilation.
- The close: avoid purely celebratory-visual slides; the final message is delivered in words as well as images.
